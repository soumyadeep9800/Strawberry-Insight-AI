# Blossom Blight

## Description
Blossom blight is an early-stage infection of the strawberry flower that prevents fruit from forming or causes severe deformities. While multiple pathogens can cause flowers to blight (including *Colletotrichum*), the most common and destructive culprit in strawberries is the gray mold pathogen, which colonizes the bloom before going dormant until the fruit ripens.

## Symptoms
Infected open flowers and unopened buds quickly dry out, turn brown, and die. Dark lesions may extend down the pedicel (flower stem), effectively girdling the flower and cutting off its nutrient supply. In humid conditions, a fuzzy gray mycelial growth may become visible on the dead blossoms. If the flower survives and pollinates, the resulting fruit will often remain small, hard, and misshapen, or will rapidly rot as it ripens.

## Causes / Pathogen
Primarily caused by *Botrytis cinerea* (Gray Mold), though *Colletotrichum acutatum* (Anthracnose) can also blight strawberry blossoms. The pathogens use the delicate, nutrient-rich floral tissues as an entry point into the plant.

## Conditions Favoring Disease
Blossom blight is heavily favored by prolonged periods of moisture (rain, dew, overhead irrigation) during the blooming period. Cool to mild temperatures (59–72°F) favor *Botrytis*, while slightly warmer temperatures favor *Colletotrichum*.

## Prevention
*   **Irrigation:** Switch to drip irrigation prior to flowering to keep blossoms dry.
*   **Canopy Management:** Ensure plants are not overcrowded so that morning dew evaporates quickly from the flowers.
*   **Weed Control:** Remove weeds to improve air circulation at the soil level.

## Management
Fungicide intervention is most critical during the bloom phase. Protecting the flowers directly prevents both blossom blight and subsequent gray mold/anthracnose on the mature fruit. Typically, sprays are applied at 10% bloom and repeated according to the chemical's label during wet weather. Dead blossoms should be removed if physically feasible.

## Hydroponic Considerations
In hydroponics, the primary risk of blossom blight comes from excessive humidity in the greenhouse causing condensation on the flowers. Ensure active air circulation with horizontal airflow fans directly targeting the flowering canopy. Avoid foliar feeding or misting while the plants are in bloom.
