# Powdery Mildew Fruit

## Description
While powdery mildew is widely recognized as a foliar disease, it also directly attacks strawberry blossoms and fruit. When the pathogen infects the fruit, it drastically impacts marketability, causing the berries to look diseased, feel hardened, and lose their sweetness.

## Symptoms
Immature, green fruits infected with mildew fail to ripen properly, becoming hardened, desiccated, and stunted. On mature fruits, the infection causes a distinct dull, matte sheen (loss of normal glossiness) and a rusty-pink or cracked appearance. The seeds become highly prominent and raised. In advanced stages, the fruit may be covered in the characteristic white, powdery fungal mycelium.

## Causes / Pathogen
Caused by the fungus *Podosphaera aphanis*. The pathogen spreads via airborne conidia that land directly on the developing fruit or flowers. 

## Conditions Favoring Disease
Optimal conditions include warm temperatures (60–77°F) and high relative humidity (above 75%), but notably requires dry plant surfaces. Free water actually washes the spores away or prevents them from germinating. Dense, shaded canopies create the perfect high-humidity microclimate for fruit infection.

## Prevention
*   **Foliar Control:** The best way to prevent fruit infection is to aggressively manage powdery mildew on the leaves early in the season; high foliar disease pressure inevitably leads to fruit infection.
*   **Cultivar Selection:** Utilize cultivars bred for mildew resistance.
*   **Climate Management:** Ensure adequate spacing and sunlight exposure.

## Management
If foliar mildew is present as the plants enter the blooming and fruiting stages, protective treatments must be applied. Sulfur, potassium bicarbonate, and synthetic fungicides can protect developing fruit. Do not wait for fruit symptoms to appear, as the aesthetic damage to the berry cannot be reversed.

## Hydroponic Considerations
Because hydroponic systems are protected from rain, fruit powdery mildew is a severe threat. High humidity without overhead washing creates a perfect storm for *Podosphaera*. Growers must maintain strict vapor pressure deficits (VPD) to control humidity. UV-C light treatments applied at night have proven highly effective in greenhouses at destroying mildew mycelium directly on the fruit without chemical residues.
