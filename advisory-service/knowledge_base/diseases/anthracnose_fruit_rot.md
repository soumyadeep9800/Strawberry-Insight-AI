# Anthracnose Fruit Rot

## Description
Anthracnose is an aggressive, destructive disease that can wipe out a strawberry crop in a matter of days under the right weather conditions. It primarily causes severe fruit rot but can also cause crown rot, root lesions, and flower blight. It is notoriously difficult to manage once established in a fruiting field.

## Symptoms
Infections on green or ripe fruit begin as small, light-colored or water-soaked lesions. These rapidly expand into highly distinct, firm, sunken, circular spots that turn dark brown to black. Under humid conditions, sticky masses of spores that are salmon, pink, or orange in color ooze from the sunken lesions. The entire fruit eventually dries up into a hard, black mummy.

## Causes / Pathogen
Caused by multiple species of the *Colletotrichum* fungus, with *Colletotrichum acutatum* being the most common culprit for fruit rot in strawberries, alongside *C. fragariae* and *C. gloeosporioides*.

## Conditions Favoring Disease
Anthracnose requires warm to hot temperatures (75–90°F / 20–32°C) and requires near 100% relative humidity for spores to germinate. The disease is explosive in rainy weather, as the pathogen relies entirely on splashing water (rain or overhead irrigation) to disperse its sticky spores from plant to plant.

## Prevention
*   **Clean Transplants:** The disease is almost always introduced to new fields via asymptomatic, infected nursery plants. Buying certified disease-free stock is the single most important preventative measure.
*   **Mulching:** Use straw mulch rather than plastic mulch. Plastic mulch acts as a springboard, allowing water droplets to splash spores high into the canopy, whereas straw absorbs the impact.
*   **Irrigation:** Exclusively use drip irrigation to eliminate water splash.

## Management
Infected fruit must be removed from the field immediately, as they act as massive spore factories. Harvesters should avoid touching healthy plants after handling infected berries, and should not work in wet fields. Fungicides (such as strobilurins) can be used preventatively during warm, wet weather, though resistance to certain chemical classes is becoming a widespread issue.

## Hydroponic Considerations
Because *Colletotrichum* spores are encased in a sticky matrix, they cannot spread via wind—they *must* have splashing water or physical contact to spread. Consequently, hydroponic systems utilizing clean source water and strict drip irrigation are highly insulated against Anthracnose outbreaks. If it appears, it was likely brought in on the transplants. Immediately bag and destroy infected plants and sanitize tools.
