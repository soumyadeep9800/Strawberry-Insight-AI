# Gray Mold

## Description
Gray mold is arguably the most devastating pre- and post-harvest disease of strawberries worldwide. It causes a rapid, aggressive rot that can decimate yields in the field and destroy harvested fruit during shipping or in the consumer's home. It affects leaves, stems, flowers, and fruits at all stages of development.

## Symptoms
Infections on foliage or green tissue initially appear as water-soaked spots. On fruit, it presents as a rapidly expanding brown, soft rot. Within a few days, the infected tissue becomes entirely covered in a thick, fuzzy, gray layer of fungal spores. When disturbed, these spores release in a visible cloud of dust.

## Causes / Pathogen
Caused by the necrotrophic fungus *Botrytis cinerea*. This pathogen has an extremely wide host range and is ubiquitous in the environment, overwintering on plant debris, in soil, and on weeds.

## Conditions Favoring Disease
Gray mold requires prolonged periods of free moisture (13 to 16 hours of leaf wetness) and thrives in cool to mild temperatures (59–72°F / 15–22°C). High humidity, poor air circulation, and cloudy, rainy weather drive severe outbreaks. Wounded or overripe fruit are particularly susceptible.

## Prevention
*   **Airflow:** Increase plant spacing and manage canopy density to promote rapid drying of flowers and fruit.
*   **Sanitation:** Promptly remove and destroy dead plant tissue, diseased fruits, and mummified berries from the growing area.
*   **Harvesting Practices:** Pick fruit frequently and avoid handling wet plants. Cool fruit immediately after harvest.

## Management
Management relies heavily on preventative fungicide applications beginning at early bloom (since many fruit infections actually start in the flower). Given that *Botrytis* is highly prone to developing fungicide resistance, rotating chemical classes is mandatory. Biocontrol agents utilizing *Trichoderma*, *Streptomyces*, and *Bacillus* strains are increasingly utilized as safe alternatives.

## Hydroponic Considerations
In hydroponics, absolute control over humidity is the primary defense against *Botrytis*. Ensure that greenhouse fans maintain continuous air movement through the lower canopy. Promptly prune dying leaves and overripe fruit, as the fungus aggressively colonizes dead organic matter before jumping to healthy tissue. Ensure nutrient solutions do not splash onto the plant crowns.
