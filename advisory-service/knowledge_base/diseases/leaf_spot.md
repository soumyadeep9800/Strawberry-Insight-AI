# Leaf Spot

## Description
Leaf spot, often called common leaf spot, is one of the most widespread foliar diseases affecting strawberries. While it primarily targets the leaves, it can also infect stems, petioles, and the calyx. Though rarely fatal on its own, severe infections reduce the plant's photosynthetic capacity, leading to diminished vigor, smaller fruit, and lower overall yields.

## Symptoms
Infections initially appear on the upper leaf surface as small, deep purple, round spots about ¼ inch (3–6 mm) in diameter. As the lesions mature, the centers die and turn gray or white, while retaining a distinct dark red or purple border. In severe cases, spots can coalesce and kill the entire leaf. Black fungal fruiting bodies may be visible in the light-colored centers.

## Causes / Pathogen
The disease is caused by the fungus *Mycosphaerella fragariae* (asexual stage: *Ramularia tulasnei*). The pathogen overwinters as mycelium or fruiting structures on infected plant debris and living leaves.

## Conditions Favoring Disease
The fungus thrives in wet, humid conditions and is highly dependent on splashing water (rain or overhead irrigation) to spread its spores. It prefers mild to moderate temperatures and poorly drained soils. Dense canopies that dry slowly are highly susceptible.

## Prevention
*   **Sanitation:** Remove and destroy infected leaves and crop debris at the end of the season.
*   **Cultivar Selection:** Plant resistant or highly tolerant strawberry cultivars where available.
*   **Airflow:** Maintain proper plant spacing and control weeds to encourage rapid drying of foliage.
*   **Water Management:** Use drip irrigation instead of overhead sprinklers to prevent water splash. 

## Management
If the disease becomes severe, fungicide applications (such as copper-based products or myclobutanil) can be used, particularly early in the season to protect new growth. Routine scouting is necessary to catch the infection before it spreads across the canopy. 

## Hydroponic Considerations
Because leaf spot relies heavily on free moisture and water splash to spread, hydroponic systems utilizing drip lines and environmental controls usually face lower risks. However, high humidity in greenhouses can still trigger outbreaks if infected transplants are introduced. Ensure excellent ventilation and avoid getting the foliage wet during nutrient top-ups or maintenance.
